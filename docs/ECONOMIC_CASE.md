# NaijaClimaGuard Economic Case

## What this document is

This is an investor and institutional decision model. It converts the cost of flooding into transparent economic scenarios without claiming that NaijaClimaGuard has already prevented a measured amount of loss.

The distinction matters:

- **Observed national flood loss** is evidence.
- **Avoided-loss percentage** is a scenario assumption until measured in a real pilot.
- **Realized savings** must only be claimed after a partner pilot can compare outcomes against an agreed baseline.

## National loss baseline

World Bank reporting on Nigeria's 2022 floods cites estimated direct economic damages of approximately **US$3.79 billion to US$9.12 billion**, with a **median estimate of US$6.68 billion**. The estimate includes impacts on livelihoods, buildings, infrastructure, productive sectors and agriculture.

## Avoided-loss scenario model

Using the World Bank median 2022 direct-damage estimate of **US$6.68 billion** as a stress-case reference:

| Scenario | Assumed fraction of direct loss avoided | Value protected in a 2022-scale event |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Conservative | 0.5% | US$33.4 million |
| Low | 1% | US$66.8 million |
| Moderate | 2% | US$133.6 million |
| Strong | 5% | US$334.0 million |
| Very strong | 10% | US$668.0 million |

These are **not realized NaijaClimaGuard savings**. They are sensitivity scenarios showing why even small improvements in warning reach, decision speed, asset movement, business continuity or agency coordination can have material economic value during a catastrophic flood year.

## Why a small percentage matters

Flood losses are very large relative to the cost of a digital decision-support layer. A platform does not need to "stop the flood" to be economically useful. It can create value by helping users act earlier or more consistently.

Examples of measurable pathways include:

- households moving documents, medicine, electronics and movable assets before water arrives;
- farmers moving livestock, seed, feed, equipment and farm records;
- businesses moving stock, changing staff travel, protecting records and activating continuity plans;
- agencies prioritising communities, delivery channels, responders and critical facilities;
- banks and insurers identifying concentrated exposure and communicating with customers before an event;
- telcos and message-delivery partners increasing successful warning delivery and acknowledgement.

## Pilot ROI formula

For a real institutional pilot, use a transparent formula rather than a generic ROI claim.

`Estimated value protected = eligible exposed value × measured action rate × estimated protection factor`

Where:

- **eligible exposed value** is the value of assets/population/operations included in the pilot;
- **measured action rate** is the proportion of target users or assets for which a verified action was taken after a relevant signal;
- **protection factor** is an agreed conservative estimate of how much of the exposed value that action could reasonably protect, preferably calibrated from partner claims/loss data.

Then:

`Pilot ROI = (estimated value protected - pilot cost) / pilot cost`

The protection factor must be disclosed and sensitivity-tested. It should not be hidden inside a single impressive-looking number.

## Agency value model

Government value should not be reduced to subscription revenue. A national or state agency can evaluate NaijaClimaGuard on:

1. time from signal to assigned action;
2. percentage of priority locations with a clear owner and action plan;
3. successful warning-delivery rate by channel;
4. acknowledgement rate;
5. number of high-risk communities reached before impact;
6. false-warning burden;
7. post-event evidence completeness;
8. estimated avoided emergency-response and recovery cost under agreed assumptions.

## Bank and insurer value model

For a lender or insurer, the economic case can be measured through:

- portfolio locations screened;
- customers/assets inside an elevated-risk footprint;
- warnings delivered and acknowledged;
- assets for which preventive actions are recorded;
- claims frequency/severity comparison where legally and statistically appropriate;
- reduction in manual risk-monitoring workload;
- API and reporting integration cost versus value of protected exposure.

NaijaClimaGuard should **not** claim automated underwriting or claim denial based on the current product.

## Business continuity value model

For enterprises:

`Continuity value = avoided downtime + protected inventory + protected equipment/data + avoided unsafe travel + faster recovery`

A pilot can measure:

- time from risk escalation to continuity activation;
- number/value of assets moved or protected;
- staff travel decisions changed;
- downtime avoided or shortened;
- time to restore normal operations.

## Commercial architecture

The product can support several revenue paths without depending on consumer subscriptions alone:

- institutional pilot fees;
- enterprise SaaS by locations/users/decision workflows;
- API/data-access contracts;
- reporting and audit/evidence modules;
- last-mile communication integrations;
- custom integration and support;
- portfolio screening for financial institutions;
- white-label or embedded decision-support components.

Any price quoted to an institution should be based on scope, number of locations/users, source integrations, delivery channels, reporting requirements and support obligations.

## Investor-safe economic headline

A defensible pitch statement is:

> Nigeria's 2022 floods caused an estimated US$6.68 billion in direct economic damage at the World Bank's median estimate. In a disaster of that scale, even a 1% reduction in avoidable loss would represent about US$66.8 million in protected value. NaijaClimaGuard does not claim to have achieved that saving yet; our pilot framework is designed to measure how much decision time, action completion and loss reduction the platform can actually create.

## What we must prove next

The economic proof stack should mature in this order:

1. demonstrate national retrospective hazard discrimination;
2. preserve prospective Riverine Watch evidence;
3. run agency/business/financial pilots with agreed baselines;
4. measure action completion and delivery evidence;
5. connect outcome/loss data where partners permit;
6. publish verified avoided-loss case studies only after real evidence exists.
